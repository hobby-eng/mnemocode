# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

- Keep every line of a private result reachable on a finite screen. A page that does not fit with the question or prompt under it, such as a share set with its heading, notices and fingerprints, is shown whole by pages first, in its colours; its end then stays above the question, without the page keys. Home and End also work as a VT220 or tmux sends them.
- Enter repeats a word, number or Unicode lookup; Escape returns to the main menu. The lookup entry exports numbered English/Traditional Chinese dictionaries, Unicode characters and codes, the complete mapping and a standalone English manual recovery guide. Full set saves them together in one folder. The same exports are available with `table --all --table-format ... --output ...`, `table --recovery-guide` and `table --word-lists-dir ...`.

- Restore and recovery prompts accept a local text file path in the same field as manual input. Exported share files keep every share line, and MNC1 records keep their format and Seedshift metadata. Unreadable files are asked for again.
- Decode keeps every share from a multiline paste or text file, including members 1 and 3 of a 2-of-3 set. Later shares are no longer mistaken for extra card codes after the first share; an unreadable line is named for correction.
- Keep encoded backups, Shamir shares and re-encode confirmations visible beside follow-up questions on the private screen. Clear the result only when moving to another page or starting the written-backup check; terminal regressions now inspect the current frame rather than only its output history.
- Encode offers the original wallet phrase beside shares as words, selected codes or both, and separately offers the English words after Seedshift. Neither choice adds the words or the original copy to exported share files. Private card operations name the printability check and rendering while they run.

### Distribution

- Terminal questions return cancellation as a value. Ctrl+C ends the command with one cancellation line and exit code 130; a share assessment cancelled at its final callback gives no live repair plan.
- Refusals carry stable module codes while retaining their existing messages, native error kinds and causes. The shared error catalog and a gate against uncoded native constructors keep terminal and browser hosts on the same contract.
- A seed phrase pasted into a record mode, format, month, command, help topic or unknown option is not repeated in the refusal. Known options keep their usual labels.
- Each release has one executable file for Linux, Windows and macOS, with Node.js, the card artwork and the SSKR engine built in; `pnpm build:executable` builds it for the current computer.
- Started without a command in a terminal, for example by a double-click on the executable file, MnemoCode shows a menu. It asks step by step, with examples, and shows in grey the command that does the same before it runs it.
- Encode in the menu offers the usual sets of Shamir shares, such as 2 of 3 or 3 of 5, and any other up to 16 shares, and writes them in the form chosen: restoring them gives that form back. Entry 4 splits the seed phrase itself into standard SSKR shares in Bytewords or as a `ur:sskr` code.
- The menu checks each of its own answers at once, such as a file name, a file dragged into the terminal, a fingerprint, an address or a share count, and asks again with one line that says why. When a command stops unexpectedly, the menu offers to start it again with the same answers; after a failed self-test it no longer offers the entries that ask for a seed phrase or a share.
- `mnemocode <command> --help` explains every option of a command with examples; `-h` gives a short summary.
- Each part of the library, such as the date search, the share set or the wallet check, has an entry point of its own without Node.js code, such as `mnemocode/core/date-search`. Another program, a browser page for example, takes only the part it needs and passes what only it has, such as its PBKDF2 or its SSKR library. A long search stops at the host's signal and gives what it found before the stop, marked as stopped, rather than failing. [Source layout](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md#modules-for-other-hosts) lists the parts.
- Each executable comes with a file of license notices for Node.js and every library it contains, and a release draft is made only after the complete CI has passed.
- GitHub signs the build provenance of every release file: `gh attestation verify` shows that a download was built from this repository by its release workflow.
- The `SHA256SUMS` of every release is signed with the maintainer's OpenPGP key, fingerprint `28FC51B1DB80DF2101128CB30EDD4814591DD095`, which GitHub also shows at https://github.com/hobby-eng.gpg. A tag creates a draft release, which the maintainer checks and signs on their own computer with `scripts/sign-release.mjs` before publishing it with `SHA256SUMS.asc` and `RELEASE-SIGNING-KEY.asc`; [Releasing](docs/RELEASING.md) describes the steps.
- More parts of the library have an entry point of their own: `mnemocode/core/candidate-list-writer` writes a candidate list as the command line does, `mnemocode/sskr/share-restore` takes a page through the restore from shares step by step, `mnemocode/export/document-formats` names the kinds of card files, `mnemocode/export/card-drawing` reads the pages of card PDFs as drawing steps for a page without Poppler, `mnemocode/export/sskr-keeping-text` says how saved shares are kept, and `mnemocode/core/candidate-file-admission` checks the size and the age header of a candidate file before a host reads it in full or asks for its key.
- The SSKR engine names its two share encodings, `ShareEncoding.CompactUr` and `ShareEncoding.Bytewords`, instead of bare numbers. Its refusals name no host: "SSKR requires exactly 32 bytes of fresh randomness." and, for either encoding, "Enter at least one SSKR share."
- The package and the executable no longer carry the font subset for the Traditional Chinese BIP39 words, which no card used.
- `re-encode` makes a backup again from one made before, with other dates, another mode, in another form, as cards or as Shamir shares, without the seed phrase being typed: it decodes the old backup as `decode` does and encodes its phrase as `encode` does, with `encode`'s options for the new backup and `--old-mode`, `--old-format` and `--old-dates-file` for the old one. With `--ask-secrets` the old backup's dates are asked right after its codes, and the phrase is shown on the private screen and encoded again only once it is confirmed, since its checksum proves nothing (MnemoCode Seedshift gives a valid phrase for every date): the person recognises it or its fingerprint (`--recognised`), or the wallet has an address or the fingerprint they name, of Bitcoin or another coin. A fingerprint has only 32 bits and is a filter, not proof: its match comes with that warning, and the phrase is accepted, not called confirmed; an address proves it. Only then is the new backup asked, with Encode's questions, unless options give it. A backup of the Original Seedshift with a valid last word takes the remembered original final word privately or through `--last-word-file`. In the menu it is entry 2, after Encode. The entries after it moved one place down, Show every command and option is 0, and Quit has no digit: Escape quits. A library entry, `mnemocode/core/re-encoding`, holds the rule for other programs (`ReEncoding`).
- Where the menu asks where a result or the shares go, All of these saves them to every place it offers at once: a record file, a QR code image and printable cards, or for shares a text file and cards, beside the screen.

### Formats

- Numbered representations are now consecutive: `colors-unicode` uses `--format 4` instead of `--format 6`.
- `colors-unicode` is written as four-digit codes instead of Private Use symbols, which many programs could not show. The symbol form can still be decoded.

### Recovery

- Shares with unreadable elements can still be used: mark each with `?` at its place, on any of the shares, such as two words on each of three shares. MnemoCode repairs all of them together: the checksums, the data the shares have in common and every share beyond the threshold settle most marks without trying anything, and the SSKR secret digest decides the rest. Before any search it shows how many combinations are open, how long they take, and which element or how many more shares would settle them; a search longer than 12 hours is asked for first, or needs `--max-tries`. In colors and Unicode codes written apart, a `?` inside a code stands for one unreadable digit, such as `#B5?0??` or `4E?0`, and the digits read are used: those of a color as bits, those of a Unicode code by trying each word whose code fits. Every phrase that passes is listed with its fingerprint, never chosen, and a wallet check keeps the matching one. `sskr-combine` names every filled-in element, by share and place, so that the written copy can be corrected. Entry 6 of the menu and `sskr-export` give the repaired shares back whole, in their own form, without showing the phrase.
- `recover-word` finds several forgotten words, written as `?`, a beginning such as `ab*`, or a few words such as `rich|rice`, up to 16,777,216 combinations, and with `--missing-word` a word missing at an unknown place. Every candidate is checked offline against a wallet check when one is given.
- `recover-word`, `recover-date` and `sskr-combine` save their candidates with `--candidates-file` as a [candidate list](docs/CANDIDATES.md) for the Discovery Scanner of the multi-chain wallet tools: encrypted with age to the one-time key the Scanner shows, or with a passphrase; without encryption only with `--plaintext-candidates`. Entry 6 of the menu offers it. The search, the list and its encryption are host-neutral modules that the Wallet Deriver and the Scanner compile unchanged.
- Entry 6 of the menu also finds codes of an encoded seed phrase that cannot be read: typed with `?` at their places, as Decode reads them. Codes typed where the words of a seed phrase are asked for, as in `recover-word`, are pointed there.
- Codes are read written apart or run together wherever they can be told apart: Unicode codes, colors and Color Unicode codes as before, and now shares in Bytewords too, four letters a word, also with commas between the words or cut by a line break; the word hints follow them. Word numbers typed run together, which cannot be told apart, are refused with how to write them, and so is a `?` for one digit in codes run together. In colors, Decode names the words that a mark leaves open rather than codes.
- The master fingerprint and the other wallet checks compute the BIP39 seed with Node's own PBKDF2, about seven times faster, with the same result.
- Every wallet check also takes an address of another coin, the coins of mhfe on their standard paths: Bitcoin Cash, Cosmos, Dash (also Platform), Dogecoin, Ethereum and EVM networks, Ethereum Classic, Injective, Litecoin, Tron, XRP and Zcash (transparent). `--coin-address` with `--coin` gives it from the command line; in the menu, after Address, "Which coin is the address for?" lists every coin, Bitcoin first, then asks for its address. The library part `mnemocode/core/coins` reads and checks them for another host; `mnemocode/core/coin-address-evidence` checks an address of one coin given itself, so that a host of one coin, such as the Deriver's Dash edition, takes that coin's module alone.
- An address, a public key or a WIF is compared with the first 20 addresses of the wallet, from `--index` on, so that it need not be the first one: `--scan-gap N` sets how many, up to 1000, and the menu asks after the address, Enter for 20. The library takes the count as `addresses` in the evidence. An address of another coin given for the wrong one is refused as not that coin's, rather than as damaged.
- Every answer of the menu that is no secret can be given as an option of its command. New options: `--scan-gap`; `--max-candidates` for `sskr-combine` and `sskr-export`; `--backup-check yes|no` for `encode` and `sskr-split`, which also takes `--heir-sheet` and `--heir-sheet-size` now; for `decode --ask-secrets`, the wallet options with `--bip39-passphrase-file`, `--encoded-fingerprint`, `--list-candidates`, `--max-candidates`, `--max-results`, `--max-tries` and `--input-kind codes|share`. A share typed into Decode takes Decode's wallet and limits to the restore. The menu's own wallet question offers every coin and the number of addresses too.
- Every feature has its self-test beside it: quick known answers before every command (BIP39 seed and master fingerprint, date patterns, codes and shares marked with `?`, the word search, the candidate list, wallet evidence and the address scan, coin addresses, the heir sheet, share cards), and its row in `mnemocode self-test` with the slower checks (the 24 English BIP39 vectors, date and word searches with a wallet, age-encrypted lists, share backup checks, shares repaired together and with dates, the heir-sheet and share-card PDFs, QR images beside a sheet). Known answers come from published vectors or from an independent implementation that first reproduces one, and every check also gets a case it must refuse. Each coin has a known answer before every command, and so does the check of a written backup, and every form of a phrase of 15, 18 and 21 words with MnemoCode Seedshift, beside those of 12 and 24 words without it. Before it splits or combines shares, MnemoCode also restores a set of the 32-byte secret of a 24-word phrase, which `scripts/verify-sskr-known-answers.py` restores without the share engine, after it has restored the official BCR-2020-011 vector. The checks before every command take less than half a second: the valid last words of a phrase are now found from its entropy, without validating 2,048 phrases.
- `MnemoCodeSelfTest` takes the checks of the features a host builds in (`features`, `MnemoCodeSelfTest.core(features)`) and optionally the host's PBKDF2.
- The menu offers printable cards with a choice of design for shares in colors and Unicode codes; before, shares in word numbers came out as cards of the default design that printed colors. For printable cards it asks the kind of file (PDF, PNG or JPEG) first, then the layout (A6, A5 or A4 sheets, or separate business cards), so that images of A4 sheets can be chosen too. Separate cards are named after their number and code, such as `01-1EAB91.pdf`, without spaces. Share sheets of every design are A6 when no size is given; the material designs came out on a business-card page before. A long reference label on a separate card of the IT design shrinks to fit, and one too long to read there is refused, instead of running off the card.
- A date search finds each phrase once. Seedshift adds the year to a word number modulo the 2,048 words, so a forgotten year found the same phrase with the year 2,048, 4,096 and 6,144 years later too, as several results; now each set of shifts is tried once, with the earliest dates, which also makes a search of a whole year about five times shorter. That holds also where the day of the latest date moves only part of the last word, at the most dates a phrase takes, and the search keeps nothing of the combinations it tried, so that one of two billion needs no more memory than a small one. Its time is now told from a phrase of its own length, which for the Original Seedshift with a replaced last word made a 12-word search look about 14 times shorter. Several phrases restored from shares are numbered "phrase 2 of 3" and, on the private screen, shown one screen each, the next on Enter. A record of the Original Seedshift with a replaced last word finds its 128 or fewer last words from the entropy rather than by trying all 2,048, about seventeen times faster.
- In the card design "Signature curves" the company name stands above the logo, which moves down: lower, the curve ran under the name.
- In the menu, finding a forgotten date digit asks how to recognise the wallet: by its fingerprint or an address, whose coin is chosen from one list of every coin, as mhfe asks it, and after an address among how many to look; with the Original Seedshift it may be left out. `recover-date --ask-secrets` asks for it after the dates when the mode needs one and no option gives it.
- Decode and Restore from Shamir shares take `?` for a forgotten digit of a date on the private screen, in Restore also together with `?` in share codes, and search the dates as `recover-date` does. Where every date gives a valid phrase they ask for the wallet's fingerprint or one of its first receiving addresses, and Restore shows the share and date combinations and their time before it searches. Decode asks for the wallet with the original Seedshift too, where it may be left out.
- On the private screen, Decode, the search for a forgotten date digit and Restore from Shamir shares show the encoded fingerprint, the fingerprint of the masked phrase that Encode and the split print, as soon as the codes or complete shares are read: a code written down or typed wrongly shows before the dates are typed. Encode and Decode show it by one rule: whenever the codes are a valid BIP39 phrase, which the Original Seedshift's are only by chance.
- On a terminal, the result of Decode and of Restore from Shamir shares gets a screen of its own: the questions and the search are cleared away, and each part stands apart under its label. The original seed phrase is numbered, four words to a row as `mhfe` shows a phrase, with a line to copy below it, and each fingerprint stands under the phrase it belongs to: the encoded one under the masked phrase (decoy), the original one under the seed phrase. Restore lists the elements it filled in as a table, a row for each share, and the progress of a search stays on one line.
- On the private screen, `decode` asks for codes without an MNC1 header whether Seedshift was used and which one, and `recover-date` which Seedshift; Decode in the menu passes the answer on as `--mode`.
- When nothing tells which of two shares is wrong, such as two that carry the same share number but differ, both are named and the person chooses which to change or leave out. The written-backup check uses a share given twice once, as Restore does.
- `recover-date` accepts `?` for each forgotten digit and up to three incomplete dates. Identical patterns are not tried twice, and a limit stops a search that is too large before it starts. Every search of the dates or of marked shares that takes longer than 12 hours on the computer is asked for first; `--max-candidates` and `--max-tries` allow one from the command line.
- Decode takes codes with `?` where they cannot be read, on its private screen, through the library part `mnemocode/core/missing-codes-search`, which another host drives the same way: a word number, a word, a Unicode code or a whole color, narrowed by a few letters (`ab*`), words joined by `|`, or a `?` for one digit. It finds them by the encoded fingerprint that Encode showed, without the dates; by the wallet's fingerprint or an address with the dates, which may hold `?` too, so that codes and dates are found together; or lists them where they are few. It says how long the search takes, and shows the codes and dates it found.
- A Shamir share typed into Decode or read from a QR code, complete or with `?`, is recognized: Decode goes on to the restore from shares with it as the first share and asks for the others; a share read from a QR code is shown as text, and the next can be read from another QR code image. Where the text could also be the codes, it asks which.
- A date to search may hold a lone `?` for a whole day, month or year (`15-?-2025`), or for a whole date not remembered at all; a few values joined by `|` (`05|15-09-2026`). A search may have up to 2,147,483,648 date combinations; one longer than 12 hours is asked for first.
- Added `recover-word`. It lists the words that can replace forgotten words and marks the candidates that match a known detail of the wallet. With `--legacy-valid-last-word` it lists the valid last words of a legacy phrase.
- After Encode shows its result on the private screen, it offers to check the backup: you type it again from what you wrote down, with the dates, and MnemoCode says whether it restores the same seed phrase.
- `encode --heir-sheet` saves one sheet for heirs, to print on both sides in A5 or A6 (`--heir-sheet-size`): what the backup is, room for a hint written by hand, the steps in the menu, and the basics for someone new to wallets. It holds no secret: no codes, no dates, no fingerprint and no places. [A guide for heirs](docs/HEIRS.md) explains the same in more detail.
- A date needs a four-digit year. `23-09-26` was read as the year 23 and is now an error.
- A record header whose version is written with leading zeros, such as `MNC01:`, is refused instead of being read as `MNC1:`.
- A damaged candidate list is refused with the reason that age gives, such as a wrong tag, in a browser too, where it was replaced by the browser's own error.
- A candidate file larger than any list can be is refused before it is read or a key is asked for: "The encrypted candidate file exceeds the supported limit of 155292688 bytes.", or 155,189,248 bytes for a list without encryption. Its age header must have exactly one recipient with the usual lengths, or it is refused with "The age header is malformed."
- `--share-file` reads shares one per line or separated by `;`, as they are typed, and joins a share that is wrapped over several lines. A file that holds a seed phrase instead of shares is refused, as a typed one is. A share written with commas between its elements is read like one written with spaces.
- The type of a testnet address is described as "testnet, …", such as "testnet, Taproot (BIP86)", for every coin, Dash Core addresses included.
- When more than 100 sets of codes with `?` fit, Decode says how many fit; before, the count stopped at 100.
- When `recover-date` cannot show every candidate, it also offers to change the dates, as Decode does.
- A message about a date no longer writes "BIP39" as "bIP39".
- Several refusals say more plainly what is wrong:
  - A split: "A split needs a threshold from 2 to the number of shares, and at most 16 shares: with a threshold of 1 no share would be checked." `--threshold` and `--shares` outside that range are refused as numbers "from 2 through 16".
  - A `--mode` or `--format` that differs from the record: "The record was made with …, not with …." and "The record holds … codes, not …."
  - A share that does not fit with the others: "Share N does not fit with the others: with it they give another phrase or none. Leave it out, or check it."
  - `--list-candidates` and `--encoded-fingerprint` name their option: "--list-candidates: a list holds at most 16 candidates; …" and "--encoded-fingerprint: these codes keep no checksum, so their encoded fingerprint cannot tell them."
  - Codes that fit no form: "These codes fit no form of an encoded seed phrase.", as for codes with `?`.
  - An address of Bitcoin, Litecoin, Bitcoin Cash or Dash that is not the address of a single key: "it is not the address of a single key; only … can be checked".
  - Color Unicode with a code missing: "Color Unicode needs two four-digit codes for every color", with the numbers of codes allowed.
  - A candidate file that the Scanner key or the passphrase does not open: "This Scanner key does not open the candidate file, or its header is damaged (…)"; one whose contents only are damaged: "The candidate file is damaged (…)".
  - Lists of choices in messages have no comma before "or", such as "12, 15, 18, 21 or 24".

### Secret handling

- MnemoCode clears its temporary copies of the phrase data after use.
- Nothing is saved over an existing file or folder. When the name given is taken, MnemoCode saves under the first free numbered name, such as `cards-1.pdf`, without spaces or brackets that a shell would read, and says so after saving; the name is settled before the seed phrase is asked, so a taken name no longer stops a command. A result named like an input of the same command is still refused.
- A file with Shamir shares now appears under its name only when it is complete, as the other exports already did. MnemoCode writes it to a private temporary file first and then gives it its final name in one step, without ever replacing an existing file. On memory cards and USB sticks formatted with FAT or exFAT, which cannot give a file a second name, an empty file holds the name until the complete one replaces it, and is removed again if that fails.
- Text, standard input and QR image files are read up to their size limit and no further, also when they come from a pipe or keep growing, instead of trusting the size the system reports.
- A QR code image may have up to 8 megapixels, a whole 3840 x 2160 screen, and up to 8 bits per sample; a larger image, or one with 16-bit samples, is refused before it is decoded, so that a small file cannot make the decoder take more than about 200 MiB.
- A QR code image read as input (`--qr-file`, `--share-qr`) is wiped from memory once its code is read, also when it is refused, as text input already was: both the file's bytes and its decoded pixels.
- What is saved of a backup, the record, the shares as text, card PDFs and images and QR images, is wiped from memory once the file is written or refused.
- A typed answer can be edited as in a shell: the arrow keys, Home and End, Ctrl with an arrow, Delete, Ctrl+K and Ctrl+W, also when a long answer wraps onto further rows. Escape alone no longer swallows the next key at a prompt for a secret.
- Where a question takes words, the words that begin like the one being typed appear below it: after one letter how many there are, from two letters the words themselves, and a warning when no word begins like it. Tab completes the word as far as those words agree, and ends it with a space once one is left. This works for the seed phrase, the words of `recover-word`, a word looked up in the table, encoded seed phrases in English words and shares in Bytewords. The hints appear only where the typed text is shown, on the private screen and in the menu. A pasted Tab still separates words as a space does. The rule is one host-neutral module, `core/word-hints.ts`, which other front ends take as well.
- `--ask-secrets` works on Linux, macOS and Windows: MnemoCode reads the answer itself instead of through `systemd-ask-password`, which exists only on Linux. Backspace and Ctrl+U edit it. Keys typed or pasted before a question appears do not answer it. A paste of several lines is one answer: words, codes and dates may come one per line, and shares pasted one per line count as separated by semicolons. A wide character at the right edge wraps onto the next row as the terminal wraps it.
- No command ends because of a wrong answer. On the private screen each answer is checked as soon as it is given, and what can be checked before the dates, such as the record header, the mode, the form and the checksum, is checked before they are asked. An answer that cannot be used is explained in one line that names at most its place, such as "Date 2" or "Share 3", and asked again; the other answers are kept. A share that cannot be read is typed again alone, and an empty answer is asked again with a hint. A result that cannot be used, such as dates that give no matching wallet or shares that restore nothing, offers what to change. Only the person ends a command early, with Ctrl+C ("Cancelled.", exit code 130) or by choosing Stop, apart from a failed self-test; Escape does nothing while an answer is typed.
- When a file cannot be saved after the result is shown, for example on a full disk, the result stays on the private screen, one line says why, and MnemoCode offers to try again, to use another name, or to skip it. Shares are shown even when saving them fails, and the passphrase of a candidate list is asked again, without a limit, until both entries agree.
- A command that asks for a secret runs on a private screen of its own in the terminal: what you type is shown there, and so is the result. When you press Enter, the screen is cleared, so that nothing stays in the scrollback; output sent to a file or a pipe goes there as before.
- With `--ask-secrets`, `decode` and `recover-date` take the record from `--input-file` or `--qr-file` and ask only for the dates. `sskr-combine` and `sskr-export` refuse dates given on the command line with `--ask-secrets`, as the other commands do.
- An on-or-off option given a value, such as `--missing-word yes`, is refused by every command instead of being read as off.
- In a terminal that says `TERM=dumb`, which cannot clear its screen, `--ask-secrets` says so, and a start without a command shows the help with that reason.
- Text read from a QR code image is shown without its control characters, so that an image cannot move the cursor or erase a line on the private screen.
- Shares can also be written in Bytewords, the standard long form in words (`--share-format words`), as other SSKR tools write them, and, for MnemoCode only, as word numbers, Unicode codes or colors as Unicode codes (`indexes`, `unicode`, `colors-unicode`). A share file saved with `--output` holds the shares in the format shown, and `sskr-combine` shows the restored backup in the form of the shares. `encode --sskr` shows the whole phrase beside the shares only with `--format`.
- Every run is protected as far as Node.js allows: core dumps are off on Linux and macOS, and MnemoCode runs under the Node.js permission model. That model allows no network, and a command that does not need them cannot write files or start other programs. The single executable starts under the model by itself, and each entry of the menu runs in a process of its own. A caller that already runs MnemoCode under that model keeps the rights it granted on purpose, such as `--allow-net`. The library has the rights of the program that uses it. Node.js enforces the model, not the operating system, so it is not a sandbox.
- Below the permission model the operating system enforces what it can, with the same needs of each command. On Linux, seccomp refuses new sockets and, where a command starts no program, starting one; Landlock refuses file writes where a command saves nothing; both hold for every thread, on kernels that can apply them to all threads at once (Landlock needs this in a newer kernel, such as Linux 7.0). On macOS no debugger can attach, and the system sandbox refuses the network and, where a command starts none, other programs. On Windows crash reports leave out the heap, extension DLLs and DLLs from network shares are refused, and a job object refuses other programs. Each boundary is confirmed before it is named: on Linux and by the macOS sandbox by trying what it forbids, on Windows by reading it back from the system; macOS confirms that no debugger can attach only by accepting the call. What a system does not offer is left out without a refusal, and the self-test says what was not available and why.
- Before a secret, MnemoCode warns when swap is not encrypted (Linux) and when a file would be saved into a folder that a cloud service synchronises.
- On Linux the temporary files of PNG and JPEG export stay in memory (`/dev/shm`) where it can be written; otherwise they go to the system's temporary folder, which is usually on the disk.
- The folder that holds the separate cards of one Shamir share can be read only by your user account, like the other export folders.
- A command refuses an output folder that cannot be written before it asks for a secret, as the menu already did. An output given as `-` or left empty is refused with "The … needs a path; - and empty paths are not accepted."

### Card exports

- The QR code that `--card-qr` prints on the sheets is also saved as a PNG image where they go: beside the PDF, or in the folder of the images or card files, one image per share for Shamir shares. Decode reads it back with `--qr-file`, Restore with `--share-qr`.
- Every collection, including A4, is one page laid out as a design proposal.
- A collection sheet has a centred heading and the slogan in the bottom line. Business cards lie on a dark page with a grey world map, and material samples on a backdrop of their own setting.
- A material sample shows a colour that the material is really made in, near its reference, laid over the photograph. Two different codes never get the same sample on one sheet.
- The optional QR code of a collection has its own corner on the same page. No separate QR page is made.
- Individual cards have no QR code.
- In the menu, "Also as printable cards" asks two questions about the files: which kind of file, PDF, PNG or JPEG, saying at once when images need `pdftocairo` and it is missing, and then how the cards are laid out, on A6, A5 or A4 sheets in one PDF or as images of the sheets, or as separate business cards in a new folder. For sheets it asks whether to add a QR code with the codes of the backup, without the extra cards, and for all cards whether to type your own details. The names and details on cards are a decoy, random unless typed.
- `--page-size business` gives separate numbered cards, one per page. `a6`, `a5` and `a4` give one sheet, and `a6` is the default for every template. The `wallet` size is removed.
- Images are made with the Poppler program `pdftocairo`. The rounded corners of a separate card are transparent in PNG.
- Separate business cards print their number before the code. No card prints the size of the set.
- Invented email and website addresses end in `.example`, so they cannot belong to a real company.
- `--cards-dir` is refused together with a sheet size.
- Each Shamir share has its own page, and a QR code holds exactly one share.
- Other programs can render the same cards through the `mnemocode/cards` entry point, which has no Node.js dependency. A card session keeps the same invented person, company and studio for every export of one working session.
- Text on business cards is aligned with its icon or logo, and a role no longer touches the name above it.
- The dated Unicode card and its options `--event` and `--events` are removed: no card design ever printed it.
- Codes written as Unicode codes (format 3) have cards, for the phrase and for its shares: one code to a card: on A6, A5 or A4 sheets in every template, and as separate cards in the business card designs. Shares in Unicode codes are not printed with the enclosure and tile materials, which have too few samples for the longest share. The color of a card only decorates it: one code always has one color, and the different codes of a set get colors that look as different as possible on every design, compared in OKLab as each design shows them. Card export now requires format 3, 4 or 5.
- Shares written as word numbers, Bytewords or a `ur:sskr` code have no cards; shares in colors or Unicode codes print one code to a card. Cards of shares in another form are refused before a secret is asked: "Shares written as words have no cards: cards print shares in colors or in Unicode codes."
- A sheet ends with a few random codes that belong to nothing: at least one, then as many as fill every row. It has at least three rows where they fit, not two long ones, with the largest cards that fit on one page, and of cards about as large the most of them, so that its cards fill the page in height and width. The rows share the height evenly, above, between and below them, and the QR code sits higher, in the corner of the heading. Without `--orientation` a sheet is turned the way that gives such a sheet. Separate cards add exactly one decoy after all real codes, including for each Shamir share; a PDF, its images and its card files of the same layout show the same cards. The QR code holds only the real codes.
- The sheet for heirs says how many words the seed phrase has, or how many codes each share has, so that the codes after them on printed cards are left out. `decode --words` and `recover-date --words` read only the codes of so many words from the start, and the menu asks for the number when codes are typed. A decode whose checksum fails names the shorter starts of the codes whose checksum is valid, by their fingerprints.
- A share is read up to its own length, which its first codes say: codes typed after it, such as the extra codes of its cards, are left out.
- Business cards take the brightness of their color as well as its hue, and a wider range of its saturation, so that pale, deep and dark colors of one set look apart. The curves design chooses its ink by the brightness too.
- The three frosted glass designs are removed: every card holds one code. There are thirteen templates.
- The A5 page size, 148 × 210 mm, gives one sheet, as A6 and A4 do.
- Saved PDFs carry no document information: no creation or modification time, no producer.
- The QR code of a sheet is at least 12 mm wide, so that its caption fits under it.
- A long name under a sample, such as the name of a finish, takes two lines at the size of the sheet instead of shrinking into small print, in every design. The line with the number and the code always stays whole.
- The concrete panels, wall tiles and parquet behind material samples are laid out around the samples: their joints run between them, and every sample sits in the middle of a panel or tile of its own, on every page size. Joints laid out by the page alone ran along the edges of some samples, and a panel two samples wide held each of them off its middle. The holes of the form ties sit where the joints cross, between the samples.
- The object in each material photograph, a tile, a switch plate, a kitchen front or a hood, sits in the middle of its sample: before, some lay up to 12 pixels of the photograph off it, against the dark backdrop of a tile. `scripts/trace-material-outlines.py` finds the object and says where a crop centres it; the tint of a tile now follows the traced tile.
- In the menu, sheets ask how they are turned: landscape, portrait, or whichever fills the sheet best (`--orientation`; without it, at least three rows where they fit, then the largest cards).
- Entry 9 of the menu and the summary of `self-test` read "Run the self-test".
- A share in Color Unicode codes written apart takes a `?` for one digit of a code, such as `E1?A`, as colors and Unicode codes take it: its color is filled in from the colors whose codes fit the digits read. Color Unicode codes are shown apart code by code, four digits each. A share whose codes are run together with a `?` among the digits is refused with what such a `?` means: a whole code; to mark one digit, write the codes apart.
- On a terminal, Unicode codes and colors as Unicode codes, which are written joined, are shown apart, separated by spaces, and then joined on one line, for copying: the encoded result, its shares and a backup restored from shares. A file or a pipe keeps the one joined line.
- A new folder for cards or images may not have a name that ends in `.pdf`, `.png`, `.jpg` or `.jpeg`, which would later stand in the way of a file of that name. A file whose name a folder has is refused with "A folder has this name; give the file another name, or a name inside that folder."
- After shares are saved, one note says how many of them restore the phrase, or with Seedshift only the masked phrase, and where each share is in what was saved. It replaces the notes "Each PDF contains ONE SSKR share…" and "Each folder is one share…".
- Separate sample cards show one design: `preview --all --cards-dir` is refused with "Separate sample cards show one design: choose one design.", before the folder is checked.
- Separate cards are made in the business card designs only. A design of material samples is a collection that only its sheet shows, with its backdrop and QR code, so it is printed on A6, A5 or A4 sheets; separate cards of it, `--cards-dir`, `--page-size business` or `--card-layout individual`, are refused before any secret is asked, and the menu offers only the business card designs for them.
